Swerve Kinematics Lab

Drive a swerve robot with two sticks and watch the math. The sticks become one ChassisSpeeds, kinematics turns it into a speed and angle for each of the four wheels, and the wheels move the robot. New here? Press Walkthrough.

    Wheel math

    Which of these does our robot use?

    MWLib's Module.runSetpoint calls optimize and cosineScale on every module, and every drive request calls discretize. The drive requests do not call desaturateWheelSpeeds; in open-loop teleop, a wheel asked for more than SPEED_AT_12V_MPS simply runs at full 12 V. Turn desaturation off to see what that does.

    Keyboard and gamepad

    WASD drive (left stick). Q turns left (counter-clockwise), E turns right (right stick). Hold Shift for half stick. You can also focus a stick and use the arrow keys. A plugged-in gamepad works too: press any button on it once so the browser sees it.

    The state RobotContainer asks for

    From stick to ChassisSpeeds, step by step

    This is MWLib's calculateSpeedsBasedOnJoystickInputs(): deadband each axis, flip the sign (stick forward reads negative, and WPILib +y is left), multiply by the max rate, then a slew limiter on vx and vy. Read more on the Swerve and Operator Interface pages.

    Things to try

    1. Press Turn robot +π/2 so the robot faces left, then push the left stick forward in Field-centric and in Robot-centric. Which one would a driver standing at the blue wall want?
    2. Spin in place with only the right stick. All four wheels point along a circle around the center, and the table shows the same speed on each: ω × 0.269 m.
    3. Turn on Show the vector sum, then drive forward and spin at the same time. The front-left and back-left wheels end up different lengths. Why?
    4. Full forward plus full spin. The fastest wheel is asked for over 7 m/s. Look at the dashed arrows, then turn off Desaturate and watch the path bend.
    5. With Optimize on, flick the stick from full forward to full back. No wheel turns; they all spin backward. Turn Optimize off, lower Steering speed to 4 rad/s and do it again.
    6. Turn off both Optimize and Cosine scaling and reverse quickly. The robot lurches the wrong way while the wheels turn. Cosine scaling is the fix.
    7. Pick Rotation lock and strafe around the hub. The heading controller spins the robot so the front always faces the hub, and the right stick does nothing.
    8. Turn off Discretize, then drive straight while spinning hard in Field-centric. The path drifts to one side. That drift is what ChassisSpeeds.discretize removes.
