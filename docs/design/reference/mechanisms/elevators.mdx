An elevator lifts a game piece straight up (or out, as a horizontal slide) on rails. It keeps the game piece at the same
angle the whole way, which makes it easy to control and fast to score at several heights. In FTC, elevators are usually
called **linear slides**.

<img src={useBaseUrl('/img/design/mechanisms/elevator.svg')} alt="Two-stage elevator with a carriage, constant-force spring, and hard stop" width="680"/>

## Principles

- **Keep the carriage light.** Every pound on the carriage slows the elevator and adds load on the motors. Move weight
  down into the robot when you can.
- **Gear for speed and holding.** The gear ratio sets how fast it moves and whether it can hold still under load. Check
  with [ReCalc's linear mechanism calculator](https://www.reca.lc/linear).
- **Counterbalance.** Constant-force springs or gas springs take the weight of the carriage so the motor can accelerate
  faster and hold position with less current.
- **Stiffness.** Rails flex and rack when extended. Use bearing blocks with little play, overlap each stage enough, and
  space the rails wide.
- **Rigging tension.** Belts, chain, or rope that go slack make the elevator jump. Add a tensioner and check it often.
- **Hard stops and sensors.** A hard stop gives a known zero. Limit switches or encoders keep the code from driving past
  the end of travel.
- **Center of gravity.** A tall elevator with a load on top can tip the robot. Limit acceleration when extended.

## Types

| Type | How it works | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous rigging | One rope or belt drives all stages together. | Smooth; the carriage reaches the top before the stages. |
| Cascade rigging | Each stage is pulled by the one below it. | Simple to rig; the top stage moves fastest, so the motor sees more load. |
| Single stage | One moving stage on fixed rails. | Simplest and stiffest; limited height. |
| Horizontal slide | An elevator laid on its side to reach out. | Reach without changing height. |
| Linear slides (FTC) | Off-the-shelf telescoping drawer slides pulled by string or belts. | Light and cheap; less stiff. |
