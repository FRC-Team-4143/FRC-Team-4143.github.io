An end effector holds a game piece on the end of an arm or elevator and places it. It has to grab quickly, hold
through hits, and let go cleanly in the same spot every time.

## Principles

- **Self-centering.** Rollers or angled surfaces should pull the piece into one known position, even when it comes
  in crooked.
- **Grip without crushing.** Compliant wheels, grippy studs, or sponge give grip on pieces of slightly different
  sizes.
- **Know what you're holding.** A beam break, proximity sensor, or motor current tells the code when a piece is in,
  and which piece it is.
- **Light.** The end effector rides on the end of an arm or elevator. Every ounce saved here makes the whole
  mechanism faster. See [Arms & Wrists](./arms.mdx) and [Elevators](./elevators.mdx).
- **Release cleanly.** Rollers that spin out, or a claw that opens wide, place the piece without dragging it.
- **One effector for two pieces.** When a game has two game pieces, one effector that handles both saves weight and
  time. Many 2023 and 2025 robots did this.

## Types

- **Roller claw:** Powered rollers pull the piece in and spit it out. Balls, cubes, tubes.
- **Pincher or clamp:** Two jaws close on the piece. Cones, tubes, hatch panels.
- **Suction:** A vacuum pump and cups hold flat or smooth surfaces.
- **Hook or fork:** A passive shape slides under the piece and lifts it. Rings, tetras, tubes.
