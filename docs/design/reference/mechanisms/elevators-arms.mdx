Elevators and arms move a game piece to a scoring height. They are often the heaviest and slowest part of the robot,
so the design choices here decide cycle time.

## Principles

- **Keep the moving end light.** Every pound at the end of an arm or on top of an elevator slows it down and adds
  load on the motors. Move weight down into the robot when you can (2910 cut their 2025 end effector from 15 to 12 lb).
- **Gear for speed and holding.** The gear ratio sets both how fast it moves and whether it can hold still under
  load. Check with [ReCalc's arm](https://www.reca.lc/arm) and [linear mechanism](https://www.reca.lc/linear)
  calculators.
- **Counterbalance.** Gas springs, constant-force springs, or surgical tubing take load off the motor so it can
  accelerate faster and hold position with less current.
- **Stiffness.** A long arm or tall elevator flexes. Use bearing blocks with little play, box tubing, and wide
  spacing between rails.
- **Hard stops and sensors.** A hard stop gives a known zero. Limit switches or absolute encoders keep the code from
  driving the mechanism past its range.
- **Center of gravity.** A tall elevator with a load on top can tip the robot. Limit acceleration when extended.

## Types

| Type | How it works | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous elevator | One rope or belt drives all stages together. | Smooth and fast; more rigging. |
| Cascade elevator | Each stage is pulled by the one below it. | Simple rigging; the top stage moves fastest. |
| Single-jointed arm | One pivot at the shoulder. | Simple; reach changes with height. |
| Double-jointed arm | Shoulder and elbow. | Reaches anywhere; harder to control. |
| Telescoping arm | An arm that also extends. | Reach and height in one; heavier joint. |
| Four-bar or six-bar linkage | Linked bars keep the end level as it moves. | Keeps a game piece level without a wrist. |
| Wrist | Rotates the end effector at the end of an arm or elevator. | Scores at different angles. |
