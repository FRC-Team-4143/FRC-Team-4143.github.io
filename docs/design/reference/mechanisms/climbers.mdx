A climber lifts the whole robot off the floor at the end of the match: onto a bar, up a rope or chain, or onto a
platform. It carries the heaviest load on the robot (the full robot weight, plus a bit of swinging), only works for a
few seconds, and has to hold on after the power is cut. The [Endgame & Climbing](../game/endgame/index.mdx) pages list
the past endgames it has to handle.

## Principles

- **Hold with the power off.** Scoring is judged after the match ends, so the robot has to stay up when the motors
  stop. Use a ratchet and pawl, a friction brake, or a lead screw that can't be back-driven. Brake mode on the motor
  alone is not reliable.
- **Spring out, winch back.** Constant-force springs, gas springs, or surgical tubing push the hooks up fast. A winch
  then pulls the robot up. A ratchet lets the spring extend freely while stopping the winch from unwinding.
- **Size the winch for the whole robot.** Spool torque is robot weight times spool radius (150 lb on a 0.25 ft radius
  is about 37.5 lb-ft). Plan for a low battery at the end of the match, and keep each motor well below stall. Rope
  wrapping on itself makes the spool bigger, which needs more torque. Then check climb time: height divided by rope
  speed.
- **Hooks that can't miss and can't fall off.** Make the hook opening wider than the bar, with a sloped lead-in, and
  add a spring latch. Check that it can actually reach the bar at full extension.
- **Mind the center of gravity.** A hanging robot swings until its center of gravity is below the hooks. Put the hooks
  above the center of gravity, or brace against the structure with a reaction arm or rollers. On a traversal, time the
  release to the swing.
- **Use the right rope.** Dyneema rope is light, strong, and barely stretches, but it is slippery, so use proper knots
  and keep it off sharp edges. Flat strap keeps the spool radius more constant.
- **Automate it.** The fastest climbs in 2022 were one-button sequences. Automation makes a complex climb repeatable
  under pressure.

## Types

- **Telescoping hook and winch:** Spring-loaded telescoping tubes raise a hook; a winch pulls the robot up. The most
  common climber, and available as kits (AndyMark Climber in a Box, WCP GreyT Telescope).
- **Elevator climber:** The scoring elevator doubles as the climber, with a gear shift or ratchet for the heavy lift.
- **Pivoting arms (traversal):** Arms rotate between rungs to climb from bar to bar, as in 2022.
- **Cage or chain grabber:** A clamp or funnel catches a moving cage or chain, then a winch or arm lifts the robot, as in
  2024 and 2025.
- **Rope spool:** A spinning drum with Velcro or hooks catches and winds up a rope, as in 2017.
- **Platform climber:** Legs or a lifter push the robot up a step, as in 2019.
- **Partner lift:** Ramps, forks, or wings that lift partner robots (2007, 2010, 2018).
- **Lead screw or rack and pinion (FTC):** Slow but strong, and doesn't back-drive, so it holds with the power off.
