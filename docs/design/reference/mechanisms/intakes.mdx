An intake gets a game piece off the floor (or out of a human player station) and into the robot. Fast cycles start
here: a robot that has to line up carefully on every piece will lose to one that can drive over a piece and own it.

## Principles

- **Touch it, own it.** The intake should grab a piece the moment any part of it touches, from any angle the robot
  will meet it. 1678 used this as the requirement for its 2019 and 2022 intakes.
- **Wider is faster.** A full-width intake forgives bad driving. A narrow intake needs a funnel or a vision assist.
- **Roller surface speed faster than the robot.** If the rollers move the piece slower than the robot drives into it,
  the robot pushes the piece away. Gear the rollers so their surface speed is well above the drivetrain's top speed
  (about twice is a common starting point).
- **Compression.** The rollers need to squeeze the piece a little to grip it. Too little and it slips; too much and
  it jams or stalls the motor. Find the number by prototyping with real game pieces, then design it into the CAD.
- **Compliance.** Compliant wheels, flex rollers, or surgical tubing grip pieces of slightly different sizes and
  survive hits.
- **Protect it.** An intake outside the frame perimeter gets hit. Make it deploy and retract, and put the weak parts
  behind the bumper.

## Types

### Over the bumper

Rollers on a moving arm reach over the bumper and pull the piece up and in. Good for balls, cubes, and most pieces.
There are several ways to get the rollers out past the bumper:

- **Slapdown:** One pivot swings the rollers down. The simplest and most common.
- **Four-bar:** Parallel links keep the rollers at the same angle as they deploy.
- **Virtual four-bar:** Two stages linked by a chain or belt unfold together, reaching far from a small stowed size.
- **Sliding (linear):** The rollers slide straight out on rails. Very fast to deploy and stiff.

### Other intakes

- **Under the bumper:** Rollers pull flat pieces under a raised bumper section. Nothing sticks out.
- **Funnel or dustpan:** A wide, low scoop with a powered roller drives pieces into one path.
- **Claw intake:** The end effector picks up from the floor. Simple, but slower to cycle.
