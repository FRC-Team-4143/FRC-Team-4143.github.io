An indexer (also called a serializer, feeder, or hopper) stores game pieces and hands them to the next mechanism one
at a time. Most jams happen here, so a simple, reliable indexer is worth more than a fast one that jams.

## Principles

- **Single file.** Line pieces up before the shooter so each shot starts from the same place.
- **Control every piece.** A piece that is free to bounce around will jam. Keep each one touching a belt, roller, or
  wall at all times.
- **Gaps between pieces.** Space the pieces out (for example, with a faster stage downstream) so two never enter the
  shooter at once.
- **Sensors.** Beam breaks or proximity sensors tell the code where each piece is, so it can stop, count, and feed
  on command.
- **Short and direct.** Every turn and handoff is a chance to jam. Use the fewest stages that work.

## Types

- **Belt or roller tunnel:** Belts or rollers on both sides carry pieces in a line. The most common design.
- **Spindexer:** A rotating plate spins pieces around and feeds them out one exit.
- **Hopper with agitator:** A bin stores many pieces, and a moving floor or wall keeps them from jamming.
- **Funnel and diverter:** Guides pieces into one path, or sends them to different mechanisms.
