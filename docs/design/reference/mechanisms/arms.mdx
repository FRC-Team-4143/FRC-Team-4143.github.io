An arm pivots a game piece up and around. It can reach over bumpers, other robots, and field elements, and score at
angles an elevator can't. A wrist on the end of an arm or elevator turns the game piece to the right angle.

## Principles

- **Torque grows with length.** The load on the shoulder is the weight times its distance from the pivot. Keep the arm
  short and the end light (2910 cut their 2025 end effector from 15 to 12 lb).
- **Gear for holding.** An arm is hardest to hold when it is horizontal. Gear it so the motor can hold that position
  without overheating, and check with [ReCalc's arm calculator](https://www.reca.lc/arm).
- **Counterbalance.** Gas springs or surgical tubing take load off the motor, so the arm moves faster and holds
  position with less current.
- **Backlash.** Play in the gearbox gets bigger at the end of a long arm. Use a final stage with little backlash (a
  large sprocket, belt, or cable capstan) and an absolute encoder on the arm itself.
- **Stiff joints.** Use wide bearing spacing at the pivot so the arm doesn't wobble side to side.
- **Plan the path.** An arm swings through space, so make sure it can't hit the robot, the bumper, or the floor on the
  way, and that the robot stays inside the height limit.

## Types

- **Single-jointed arm:** One pivot at the shoulder. Reach and height change together.
- **Double-jointed arm:** Shoulder and elbow can reach anywhere, but are harder to control.
- **Telescoping arm:** An arm that also extends, for reach and height in one.
- **Four-bar or six-bar linkage:** Linked bars keep the end level as it moves, without a wrist.
- **Wrist:** Rotates the end effector to score at different angles.
