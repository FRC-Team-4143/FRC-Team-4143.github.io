Boxes have flat sides, so they can be squeezed between rollers or clamped, and some games ask robots to stack them.
Robots that handle them well tend to have:

- **A wide roller intake** that pulls the box in square to the robot, even from a corner.
- **A light gripper on a fast elevator**, with the ground intake kept separate when possible.
- **A way to square up the box** before lifting or stacking, so it doesn't slip or tip.

## FRC 2003 Stack Attack (storage bins)

  img="2003-bin"
  name="Storage bin (2003)"
  specs={[
  ["Size", "24.25 in x 17.25 in x 15.75 in tall"],

  ]}
  source="https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frcarchive/2003-game-manual.pdf"

/>

Robots pushed, knocked down, and stacked plastic bins on a ramped field. There are few online write-ups, but the game manual shows the bins and the scoring rules.

:::info FTC
FTC (FIRST Tech Challenge) robots are smaller, and so are the game pieces, but the mechanisms work the same way. FTC teams share engineering portfolios and CAD instead of Behind the Bumpers videos. The [gm0 robot gallery](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/appendix/gallery.html) lists them by season.
:::
