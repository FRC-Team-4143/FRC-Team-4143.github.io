A fixed bar or a ladder of rungs is the most common endgame. Robots that climb them well tend to have:

- **Hooks that are hard to miss.** A wide opening with a lead-in, sized for the bar (1.66 in outside diameter for the
  1¼ in pipe used in 2018, 2020, and 2022), and often a spring latch so the hook can't come off.
- **A strong winch or elevator that holds with the power off**, using a ratchet or brake.
- **A plan for the center of gravity.** The robot hangs below its hooks, so it swings and tips unless the hook sits
  above the center of gravity or something braces against the structure.

## FRC 2011 Logomotion (minibot pole)

Each robot carried a small minibot (up to a 12 in cube, built from FTC parts) and launched it up a vertical pole in the
last 10 seconds. The first minibot to the top scored 30, then 20, 15, and 10. Speed was everything: the best minibots
were light, with magnets or wheels pressed against the pole.

## FRC 2010 Breakaway (tower)

Robots hung from a bar on one of the two center towers for 2 points, and a robot hanging from another robot scored 3,
so teams built robots that could carry a partner up with them.

## FRC 2004 FIRST Frenzy (chin-up bar)

Robots hung from a bar at midfield, about 10 ft up, for a 50-point bonus each.

:::info FTC
FTC (FIRST Tech Challenge) robots are smaller, but they hang and climb the same ways. FTC teams share engineering portfolios and CAD instead of Behind the Bumpers videos. The [gm0 robot gallery](https://gm0.org/en/latest/docs/appendix/gallery.html) lists them by season.
:::

## FTC 2018-19 Rover Ruckus (lander latch)

Robots latched onto a bracket on the lander and hung off the ground for 50 points. Many started the match hanging and
lowered themselves in auto. Lead screws became popular because they hold with the power off.
