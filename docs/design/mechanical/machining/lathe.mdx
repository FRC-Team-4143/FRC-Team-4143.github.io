A lathe is a versatile machine that shapes material such as metal, wood, and plastic by spinning the workpiece against a
cutting tool. Lathe operations include turning, which produces cylindrical shapes; facing, which produces flat surfaces;
and taper turning, which produces tapered shapes. Lathes are fundamental to manufacturing, offering precision and
flexibility for a wide range of parts.

## Lathe Safety

A lathe is a dangerous piece of industrial equipment, and safety must be considered during operation. Review the
general [shop safety rules](./safety) first.

:::danger Hazards

**Entanglement** (major risk)
- **Harm:** Being pulled into the machine can cause serious lacerations, friction burns, broken bones, and death.
- **Cause:** Usually **loose clothing, hair, or jewelry**.
- **Prevention:** Confirm all **hoodie strings** are tucked inside your hoodie and will not come free.

**High Rotational Energy** (moderate risk)
- **Harm:** Lacerations and broken bones from being struck by a rotating object.
- **Cause:** Contact with the chuck, **leaving the chuck key in the chuck**, or an improperly secured workpiece.
- **Prevention:** Work slowly, and think through your actions and the placement of your hands during operation.

**Flying Debris** (minor risk)
- **Harm:** Minor lacerations and eye injury from metal shavings, parted-off pieces, or shattered tooling.
- **Prevention:** Always **wear safety glasses** near an operating lathe, and position your body to the side of any
  part you are parting off.

**Sharp Edges** (minor risk)
- **Harm:** Minor lacerations from metal shavings, cutting surfaces, and machined edges.
- **Prevention:** Never handle metal shavings with bare hands; use cleaning tools and gloves. Sand or file machined
  parts to remove sharp edges, either on the lathe or with other grinding equipment.

:::

:::warning Gloves
**Gloves must never be worn while the lathe is running** due to the risk of entanglement. Only put on gloves to handle
parts once the spindle has come to a complete stop.
:::

### Workpiece Setup

The workpiece (or stock) is the material that will be turned. To properly chuck the material, make sure all 3 jaws of
the chuck are fully engaged with the stock.

#### Material Supports (Optional)

Use additional support to brace longer stock, or to keep stock stable during precise or aggressive turning operations.
Extra support can be added by center drilling a hole into the end of the stock.
