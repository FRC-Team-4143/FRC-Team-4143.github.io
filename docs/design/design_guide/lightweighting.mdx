Lightweighting is a key design strategy for improving performance, efficiency, and maneuverability. By removing
underutilized material, we reduce the weight of components without compromising their strength and durability. Cars,
rockets, and robots all benefit: faster acceleration, better handling, and less strain on motors and other mechanical
systems.

## Material Selection

Choose lightweight materials such as aluminum, polycarbonate, plastic, or carbon fiber. Each offers a different balance
of strength, weight, and cost.

| Material | Strengths | Typical Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminum (6061) | Good stiffness-to-weight, easy to machine | Chassis, brackets, structural components |
| Polycarbonate (Lexan) | Impact resistant, transparent | Covers, lightweight end effectors |
| 3D-printed plastics (ABS, Nylon) | Fast to iterate | Rapid prototypes and low-load parts. **Orient layers for strength.** |

:::tip Guidelines
- Default to aluminum for structure.
- Use polycarbonate where impact resistance or visibility matters.
- 3D print for fast iteration, and validate the part before putting it under load.
:::

See [Materials](./materials) for stock sizes and recommended uses.

## Design Techniques

Use these techniques to remove unnecessary mass while keeping parts fit for their loads.

### Lightening Holes

Remove material from low-stress regions (rounded cutouts, hex patterns, or slots) while keeping webs and fastener areas
intact. Use smooth radii to avoid stress risers.

- **When to use:** Long chassis rails, sheet-metal brackets, battery trays, and non-load-bearing covers.
- **Example:** Perforated side rails on a robot frame that shave weight without reducing stiffness in mounting zones.

### Hollowing or Pocketing

Machine or 3D print internal pockets or bores into thick sections (pockets, counterbores, internal cavities) instead of
using solid blocks. Keep wall thickness sufficient for stiffness and fastener engagement.

- **When to use:** Thick spacers, long standoffs, and motor or gearbox housings where internal volume is unused.
- **Example:** A pocketed aluminum motor mount where the outer flange stays solid for fasteners, but the central web is
  relieved to cut weight.

### Ribs and Gussets Instead of Full Thickness

Replace uniform-thickness walls with thinner skins supported by ribs or gussets. This maintains bending stiffness while
reducing material. Align ribs with the principal load directions.

- **When to use:** Large panels, lightweight arms, intake ducts, and 3D-printed parts that need stiffness but not full
  mass.
- **Example:** A thin polycarbonate cover stiffened with ribs rather than a thicker plate.

### Structural Optimization and Validation

Identify low-stress regions for material removal using hand-sketched bending diagrams or FEA (finite element analysis).
FEA is an advanced option used in industry that 4143 does not currently use.

- **When to use:** Major weight-reduction efforts on critical components (arms, structural members, or other major
  components).
- **Example:** Prototype two versions of an arm (one solid and one pocketed), then compare deflection and fatigue under
  representative loads.

:::warning
Always validate lightweighted parts with physical tests (load test and deflection check).
:::

### Guidelines

- Keep fastener zones, bearing holes, and interfaces at full thickness.
- Avoid removing material in line with likely impacts and between connected pulleys.
- Add fillets and avoid sharp corners to limit stress concentrations.
- Prototype and test lightweighted parts early, and iterate.

## Example: Simple Lightening in Onshape

**1. Identify the part to lighten.**

<img src={Lightening_Step1} width="400" alt="Part selected for lightening"/>

**2. Start a sketch.**

- Bound the area to be lightened. This can be the whole part or specific sections.
- Draw lines along the expected stress paths. The lines do not have to form an enclosed shape.
- Finish the sketch.

<img src={Lightening_Step2} width="400" alt="Lightening sketch with stress lines"/>

**3. Use the Part Lightening tool.**

:::note
Part Lightening is an Onshape FeatureScript and must be added to your account before use.

- Select the face to remove material from.
- Select the lightening sketch.
- Adjust the wall and rib thickness.
- Select the tool diameter (0.125" or 0.25").
- Adjust the cut depth for pocketing, or cut all the way through for lightening holes.

<img src={Lightening_Step3_3} width="400" alt="Weight reduction notice"/>

**4. Review the lightened part for issues.**

- Check bearing and fastener holes for correct thickness and diameter.
- Check for any stress paths that were not considered.
