## What Is Machining?

Machining is the art of precisely removing material from a larger piece. Machinists use lathes, milling machines,
grinders, and saws to cut and shape metal, plastic, composites, and wood.

- **CNC (Computer Numerical Control):** a computer program moves the machine.
- **Manual machining:** a person moves the machine's controls.

## Tools

We mostly use **drills** and **endmills**. Look at the ends: a drill comes to a point, and an endmill is flat (or
rounded, for a ball endmill) so it can cut sideways.

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| 0.159" drill | 10-32 tap hole |
| 0.201" drill | 1/4-20 tap hole, and 10-32 bolt clearance hole |
| 1/8" endmill | Can be weak. Works well on polycarbonate. |
| 1/4" endmill | Perfect for the Tormach. Cut less than 3/8" per pass. Use it to bore larger holes, like 1.125" bearing bores. |

- Holes are easy to make bigger with a hand drill.
- Peck drill if the material is thicker than 0.25".
- Bigger machines can use bigger tools.

See [Tools & Offsets](../mechanical/cam/tools.mdx) for the tool numbers on each machine.

## Safety

- **Eyes:** safety glasses.
- **Ears:** hearing protection.
- **Hands:** parts and tools are sharp, and tools spin fast.
- Keep the doors closed and the guards on the machine.
- Make sure everyone in the room is wearing eye and ear protection.
- Keep the coolant in the machine.

## Practice Part Workflow

1. **Model the part in Onshape**, make a drawing of it, and print the drawing.
2. **Export the part as a STEP file** (STEP for CNC, STL for 3D printing).
3. **Open the STEP file in Fusion** with **Open from my computer**.
4. **Switch the workspace to Manufacture.**
5. **Create a setup** (see below), then add operations.
6. **Post process** the setup to create the `.nc` program.

:::tip Fix the mouse controls
Fusion's mouse controls are different from Onshape's. Click your name in the upper right corner, open
**Preferences**, and change **Pan, Zoom, Orbit** to **Tinkercad**.
:::

### Coordinates

Fusion uses a right-hand coordinate system: **Z** is the tool direction, and **X** is usually the part's long axis.

### Setup

Go to **Setup > New Setup**.

- **Stock:** set **Stock Offset** to **No Additional Stock**. The brown shaded box will disappear.
- **Post Process:**
    - **Program number:** the name of the Onshape part.
    - **Program comment:** warnings or setup notes for whoever runs the program, who may not be familiar with it.
    - **WCS offset:** which corner of the vise the part origin is at. **This is very important.**

| G-code | WCS offset |
|---|---|
| G54 | 1 |
| G55 | 2 |
| G56 | 3 |
| G57 | 4 |

:::caution
When turning on the machine, click **Ref Z**, then **Ref X** and **Ref Y**. Don't click the buttons under the
electrical tape: they will mess up the G54 offsets.
:::

### Common Operations

- **Drilling**
- **2D Adaptive Clearing**
- **2D Contour**
- **Boring**

#### Drill

1. **Tool tab:** select the tool and check the speeds and feeds.
2. **Geometry tab:** select the holes.
3. **Heights tab:** select **Drill tip through bottom** and set **Break-through depth** to 0.1".

#### Bore (2D > Bore)

1. **Tool tab:** select the tool and check the speeds and feeds.
2. **Geometry tab:** select the inner surface of the bore.
3. **Heights tab:** set **Bottom Height** to -0.1".

### Post Process

Right click the **setup** and click **Post Process**, then select **Post** to save the `.nc` file to a USB drive.

:::warning Common problem
Right click the **setup**, not an operation. Otherwise you get a program with only one tool.
:::

| Machine | Post type |
|---|---|
| Woodshop router | Carbide 3D |
| Tormach mill | Tormach PathPilot |
| McBride & Shoff mill | Fanuc |

### Running the Part

Use the probe to find the part's location in the machine.

:::danger
Never let the spindle turn on while the probe is installed. It will wreck the probe's wires.
:::

The routers work similarly, except the part sits on the **positive side of the X and Y axes** and **Z zero is on top
of the part**.

## Pre-Run Checklist

- Are the stock settings right?
- Are the coordinates rotated correctly? (Z is the tool direction, X is the long axis.)
- Does the zero point make sense?
- Is the right WCS selected, and does it agree with the zero point and the machine?
- Are the tool numbers correct, and do they agree with the machine's tool table?
- Are the speeds and feeds reasonable? (About 5,000 RPM, under 20 in/min.)
