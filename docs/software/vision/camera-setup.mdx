# Setting Up the Cameras

Every vision camera is set up from the camera web page that runs on the [Jetson](../hardware/jetsons.mdx).
Connect to the robot's network and open:

- Varsity: [http://10.41.43.200:5800](http://10.41.43.200:5800)
- JV: [http://10.44.23.200:5800](http://10.44.23.200:5800)

The page has a tab down the left side for each step below. A new camera goes through them in this order: **Sources**,
**Calibration**, **Pipelines**, then check the **Field Layout** once a season. See
[How Our Vision System Works](./pipeline.mdx) for what each piece does.

:::warning Units
Every distance on the camera page is in **meters** and every angle is in **radians**, the same as in our
robot code. The one exception is the calibration board's square size, which is in millimeters because
that's how it's printed.
:::

:::note About the screenshots
The screenshots on this page were taken from the camera page's own code running with made-up cameras
(serial numbers 40123456 and 40123457), so the camera preview is black. On the robot it shows the live
picture.
:::

## Sources

The **Sources** tab lists the cameras the Jetson can find on its network and USB ports, by serial number.
**Connected** says whether the camera is answering right now. Click a camera, then:

1. Tick **Enabled**.
2. Set its **Frame Rate** in frames per second (30 is the default).
3. Pick a **Resolution**. The full size (1280x960 on our cameras) is the sharpest. The smaller ones
   (640x480, 426x320, 320x240) combine neighboring pixels into one ("binning"), which makes the picture
   faster to process and brighter, but tags have to be closer to be found.
4. Pick its **Calibration File**, once it has one (next step). A calibration is made at one resolution,
   so recalibrate if you change it.
5. Press **Save**. **Reload** throws away changes you haven't saved.

Only enabled cameras get a pipeline when the vision app starts.

![The Sources tab with a camera selected](/img/software/vision/camera-web-sources.webp)

## Calibration

A camera's **calibration** describes its lens: the focal length, the center of the picture, and how much
the lens bends straight lines near the edges. The AprilTag detector needs it to turn a tag's size in the
picture into a distance. **Every camera needs its own calibration**, even two of the same model, and it has
to be redone if the lens is changed, refocused or bumped.

You need a printed chessboard with **7 × 10 inner corners**. Measure one square's edge with a ruler; the
default is 22.86 mm (0.9 in).

1. On the **Calibration** tab, enter the square's **Edge Length** in millimeters and choose the camera
   (cameras are listed as `basler_<serial number>`).
2. Press **Start**. The badge next to the title changes to **Running**, and the camera's picture appears
   under **Debug Image** on the left.
3. Hold the board in front of the camera and move it around slowly: near and far, in every corner of the
   picture, and tilted in different directions. Hold it still for a moment in each spot so the picture is
   sharp.
4. Watch **Session Info**: **Samples** counts the board pictures taken so far.
5. Watch the **Coverage** bars. **X min**, **X max**, **Y min** and **Y max** fill as the board reaches the
   left, right, top and bottom edges of the picture, **Skew** as you tilt it, and **Scale** as you bring it
   closer so it fills more of the picture. A bar turns green at 85%, orange at 50% and is red below that.
   Keep going until they are green, or at least orange for Skew and Scale.
6. Check the **Reprojection error**. It is how many pixels the solved lens model is off by, on average, when
   it predicts where the board's corners should be. Under about 0.5 px is good. **Status** says **Ready**
   once there is a result to save.
7. Press **Complete** to save the calibration file, or **Stop** to throw it away and start over.

![A calibration in progress, with good coverage and a 0.31 px reprojection error](/img/software/vision/camera-web-calibration.webp)

Then go back to **Sources** and pick the new calibration file for that camera. Calibration files can also
be uploaded or downloaded in the **Calibration Files** box at the bottom of the tab.

:::tip
Most bad calibrations come from too few board positions near the edges and corners of the picture. That
is where the lens bends straight lines the most, and where tags often are during a match.
:::

## Pipelines

Each enabled camera has a **pipeline**: its settings for the camera driver and the detectors. Pipelines
are named `basler_<serial number>`. Pick one on the left; the **Camera Preview** shows its picture, and the
**Stream Source** box picks between the raw picture and the detector's debug image (tags outlined, with
their IDs). Press **Save** when you're done; **Reset** and **Reload** undo changes you haven't saved.

![The Pipelines tab: camera pose, camera settings and the AprilTag detector](/img/software/vision/camera-web-pipelines.webp)

### Camera pose

The **camera pose** says where the camera is mounted on the robot, measured from the center of the robot on
the floor:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| x | Meters forward from the robot's center (negative is behind it) |
| y | Meters to the left of the robot's center (negative is to the right) |
| z | Meters up from the floor |
| Roll | Radians the camera is rolled about its forward axis |
| Pitch | Radians the camera is tilted. Positive pitch points the camera **down** |
| Yaw | Radians the camera is turned left from straight ahead (π/2 ≈ 1.571 is facing left, π ≈ 3.142 is facing backward) |

The proxy client uses this to move each camera's pose to the center of the robot before it sends it to the
roboRIO. An error here shows up directly as an error in the robot's position. If the robot's pose jumps
when a different camera sees a tag, check that camera's pose first.

:::tip Measuring angles
It is fine to measure the camera's angle in degrees with a protractor or from CAD. Convert it before you
type it in: radians = degrees × π / 180. A camera tilted 15° down has a pitch of about 0.262 rad.
:::

### Exposure and gain

- **Exposure** is how long each picture collects light. Short exposures keep tags sharp while the robot is
  moving fast; too short and the picture is too dark for tags to be found.
- **Gain** brightens the picture electronically. It makes a dark picture usable but adds noise, so turn
  exposure up first and gain second.

Both are sliders under **Camera Settings**: gain from 0 to 1, exposure from 500 to 10000.

Set them on the field (or under similar lights) with the robot moving, and watch the debug image: tags
should be sharp black-and-white squares, not gray or smeared.

### AprilTag detection

Under **Detection Pipelines**, open **Apriltags** with the **+** on the right:

- The checkbox turns the AprilTag detector on for this camera.
- **Tag Family** is `tag36h11` for FRC.
- **Tag Size (m)** is the edge length of the tag's black square, in meters. FRC tags are 0.1651 m (6.5 in).

### Object detection

**Object Detection** has the settings for the game piece detector: the **model**, the **confidence
threshold**, and the **IoU threshold**. Trained models (`.onnx` files, or `.trt` TensorRT engines already built for this Jetson) are uploaded on the
**Models** tab. See
[Object Detection](./obj_det.mdx).

## Field Layout

The **Field Layout** tab lists where every AprilTag is on the field. The detector can only use tags that are
in this list.

Each season, WPILib publishes the official layout as a JSON file (in the
[allwpilib repository](https://github.com/wpilibsuite/allwpilib/tree/2026/apriltag/src/main/native/resources/edu/wpi/first/apriltag)).
Load it with the **Load JSON** button: each tag becomes an entry named `tag_<ID>`, with its x, y and z
in meters and its roll, pitch and yaw in radians. You can also add or edit a single tag by hand, which is
useful for a practice field whose tags aren't quite where the official drawing says. Use
**+ New Position** to add one, the pencil to edit, and the bin to delete.

![The Field Layout tab after loading the 2026 field](/img/software/vision/camera-web-field-layout.webp)

## Status

The **Status** tab shows every program the vision app is running, in two groups:

- **application** has a driver and an AprilTag detector for each enabled camera, named after the camera
  (for example `/basler_40123456/driver`).
- **daemon** has the programs that always run: the camera web page itself (`camera_web`), the
  `proxy_client` that talks to the roboRIO, the video streamer and the calibration program.

Each program shows its state, CPU and memory use, and how many times it has **Restarted**. A restart
count that keeps climbing means that program keeps crashing. The buttons on each row start, stop and
restart one program. **Launch** and **Shutdown** start and stop a whole group. After enabling a new
camera, shut down and launch the **application** group so the camera gets its pipeline.

![The Status tab with two cameras running](/img/software/vision/camera-web-status.webp)

## Checking it on the robot

Once a camera is set up:

1. Point the robot at a tag and open AdvantageScope or Elastic.
2. Check the dashboard alerts. MW-Lib raises "Proxy Server: Lost connection to ..." when a Jetson stops
   talking to the roboRIO, and "Proxy Server: Lost camera '...'" when a camera hasn't sent anything in the
   last 2 seconds (which also happens when it can't see any tags).
3. Look at the `Proxy/TagSolutions` log: the poses should appear while a tag is in view.
4. Put the robot at a measured spot on the field and compare the camera's pose to where the robot really is.
   See [Using Vision in Robot Code](./robot-code.mdx).
