# Computer Vision
Computer vision is a subset of algorthims and techniques that allow computers to interpret and understand visual information from the world, such as images and videos. The main goal is to develop methods to detect and track objects, recognize patterns, and extract meaningful information from visual data.
In the context of FRC, computer vision is often used to help robots identify and interact with game pieces, and or field elements. It helps the robots better understand the environment around them, enabling more autonomous and intelligent behavior.

## Vision
- Give each camera tag solution to the pose estimator in `LocalizationSubsystem`
    - Use `addVisionMeasurement(...)` with the pose the camera saw, **the time the picture was taken**, and the standard deviations
- Choose how much to trust the cameras
    - The starter standard deviations of 5.0 mean "ignore the cameras". Pick better numbers
- Drive more than 5 meters, stop, and compare your estimate with the true pose on the AdvantageScope 2D field
    - Your estimate should stay within 5 cm and 1 degree of where the robot really is
## Aiming
- Add an `AIM` state to the drivetrain
    - Turn in place to face the goal at (4.0, 4.0), using your pose estimate
    - Use a PID with continuous input, since headings wrap around
- Write `isAimed()`: true only when the error is small **and** the robot has stopped turning
- Create an `aim()` command that sets the `AIM` state while the left bumper is held
- Tune `AIM_KP` and `AIM_KD` so the robot gets there quickly without swinging past
