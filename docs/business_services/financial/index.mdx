# 💰 Financial
A robotics team spends money on robot parts, tools, electronics, competition registration, travel, apparel, marketing,
and meals. Business Services keeps track of what comes in, what goes out, and what is still owed, so the team always
knows whether it can afford what is next. Bank balance alone doesn't answer that: unpaid bills and upcoming expenses
matter just as much.

---

## Account Types
| Account Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Assets** | What we have | Cash, bank account, robot, tools, 3D printer, computers, supplies, money owed to us |
| **Liabilities** | What we owe | Unpaid parts bills, credit card balances, unpaid registration, hotel bills, vendor invoices |
| **Fund Balance** | What is left after what we owe | Our accumulated financial resources |
| **Revenue** | Money coming in | Sponsor contributions, fundraising, donations, grants, team fees, school support |
| **Expenses** | Costs of running the team | Robot parts, competition fees, travel, meals, supplies, team shirts, software |

As a nonprofit team, the question isn't *"Did we make a profit?"* but *"Did our available funds increase or
decrease?"*

---

## The Accounting Equation
**Assets = Liabilities + Fund Balance**

What we have equals what we owe plus what we have left.

---

## Double Entry Accounting
Every transaction has two sides: a **debit** to one account and an equal **credit** to another. Money coming into the
bank is a debit to Cash; money going out is a credit to Cash, with the matching debit going to the expense it paid for.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Donation received | Cash | Fundraising |
| Robot supplies purchased | Supplies | Cash |
| Meal reimbursement | Meals | Cash |

See [Tracking Transactions](./tracking-transactions.mdx) for what to record for each transaction.
